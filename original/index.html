You enter the world on a breath. You leave when your breathing stops. Every breath you take in between shapes your life.

Breathing is the most important thing you do because everything else in your life depends on it. If you can breathe deeper and fuller, then your experience of living becomes deeper and fuller.

Most people aren't aware of their own breath. You breathe 20,000+ times each day—more than anything else you consciously do. Breathing sits upstream of your heart rate, blood chemistry, sleep quality, wakefulness, reaction time, and recovery. You breathe quietly while you work and rest. Your breath responds to posture, stress, air quality, training, and whether you breathe through your nose or your mouth.

Breath comes before thought because your brain doesn't work without it. Breath comes before movement because your muscles can't contract without it. Breath comes before emotion because your nervous system is regulated by breathing rate, depth, and rhythm.

You breathe best through your nose.

Your nasal passages—two tiny slits no larger than grains of rice beneath your eyeballs—filter the bullshit of modern life, warm and humidify air, and provide the necessary resistance that actually inflates your lungs. Your nose is the front door to your life.

Mouth breathing is the back door. It bypasses these systems to maximize oxygen delivery right now. Most people mouth-breathe throughout the day and aren't even aware of it. They mouth-breathe because they can't properly breathe through their nose. They call it "congestion," "allergies," or "stuffiness." They accept it as normal. Shallow becomes the baseline.

Ancient yogis say you only get so many breaths in your lifetime. So the first and foremost goal of any individual is to breathe as deeply and fully as possible—because the quality of your breath determines the quality of your life.

In yoga, a kriya is a single, repeatable action with a mechanistic result. A habit. You already perform kriyas every day: brushing your teeth, flossing, purifying your water.

But because you can go two months without food, three days without water, and about three minutes without air, the very first kriya is clear breath. Everything depends on whether you can mechanically breathe.

That's why true yogis use neti. Add a small amount of salt, fill the neti pot with warm water, and rinse once through each nostril. Do it before you brush your teeth. Do it in the shower before bed.

This isn't some secret practice. It may not be common in America, but it's been around for thousands of years in India because it works. In fact, every serious lineage—from Christianity to surfing—centers on breath. Cleaning your nasal cavity is basic, essential hygiene.

The Original Neti Pot™ is original by design. The goal was to build the best-in-class standard.

As warm saline water flows into one nostril and out the other, it flushes mucus, dust, pollen, and pollution you can't avoid in modern life. Even if you don't smoke, everything in your environment—from your car to your neighbor—does. If the light is just right, you can see thousands of microscopic specks hanging in the air. Those particles accumulate in your nasal cavity, behind your eyes, in front of your brain, quietly reducing clarity and dulling consciousness.

All you have to do—like cleaning the filter of a fish tank—is flush your nose clean.

You want warm saline water because salt reduces inflammation in your nasal lining, making it physically wider so it can draw in more air. You want just enough salt to roughly match your blood so it feels right. Don't add other ingredients. Unlike your gut, your nasal surface prefers to be clean and empty—its primary job is filtration.

Material matters. A lot.

Your nasal cavity sits directly beneath the blood–brain barrier. Don't use plastic neti pots. Plastic degrades and sheds particles, especially in saline. Ceramic and porcelain work, but they're fragile and slippery when wet. Copper is best. It's naturally antimicrobial, durable, and has been valued across ancient traditions for its healing properties.
