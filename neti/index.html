The best tool for the most important habit.

You enter this world on a breath. You leave when breathing stops. The shape of your life is determined by the shape of every breath you take in between.

Breathing is the most important habit. So it makes sense that one of yoga's cleansing practices begins with the nose. It's called jala neti.

A kriya is a habit that changes you inside to out. Jala neti is simple: rinse your nose with salt water. The older yogis I met in Bihar did it first thing in the morning. Before anything else.

Fill the pot with lukewarm saline. Use distilled or sterile water, or tap water you've boiled and cooled. Never use water straight from the tap. Follow the nasal saline mix's directions for how much water to use.

Lean over the sink, tilt your head to one side, and breathe through your mouth. Rest the spout in the upper nostril and pour gently. Gravity takes the rinse through and out the lower nostril. Repeat on the other side.

Saline is gentler than plain water. The rinse helps wash away mucus, dust, and pollen. Even when I don't think I need a rinse, that first breath afterward can surprise me.

This is a ritual. When air enters and fills you, that's new spirit for life. The more freely and fully I breathe, the more alive I feel. Inside to out.

I sourced this pot through friends I met at the Bihar School of Yoga, an ashram in Bihar, India, known for kriya and tantra. The older yogis there used pure copper pots to breathe in the day. That's why I wanted this pot in pure copper, not plastic or ceramic.

Cleaning your nose isn't a common habit in the United States. We pay attention to what we eat and drink. Why not take the same care with what we breathe?

The pot is hand-shaped from pure copper and comes with a microfiber towel. After each use, wash the pot with mild soap and safe water. Dry the outside with the towel, then set the pot upside down to air-dry completely. Drying helps slow oxidation. The copper may still darken over time. That's natural.

Warning: once you start, you may never want to stop.

Get your Pure Copper Neti Pot and try the ritual for yourself. See what changes when you start with the breath. Breathe deeper.

Questions

What is jala neti?

Jala neti is the yogic practice of rinsing your nose with salt water. A neti pot uses gravity to wash away dust, pollen and mucus.

How do I use it?

Fill the clean pot with lukewarm saline. Lean over a sink, tilt your head sideways and breathe through your mouth. Pour gently into the upper nostril so water flows out the lower one. Repeat on the other side. Ask a health professional how often to rinse for your needs.

What water and salt should I use?

Use distilled or sterile water, or tap water that has been boiled and cooled. Never use water straight from the tap. Use a nasal saline mix and follow its water measurements. Read the CDC guidance.

Is nasal rinsing right for everyone?

Do not rinse if your nose is fully blocked. Ask a health professional first if your immune system is weak, or before using it with a child. Stop and get medical advice if you have pain, nosebleeds, fever or headaches.

Why copper?

In Ayurveda, India’s traditional system of medicine, copper is called tamra. Its long association with healing includes copper vessels used to store water.

Research has found antibacterial effects when water sits in copper pots for hours. That does not establish healing benefits for a neti pot or make tap water safe for nasal rinsing. We choose copper for a lasting tool rooted in that tradition.

Always use distilled, sterile, or boiled-and-cooled water, then wash and dry the pot after use.
