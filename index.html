You enter the world on a breath. You leave when breathing stops. Everything in between is shaped by how you breathe. You breathe 25,000 times each day. Breath comes before every thought, movement, and emotion.

You are designed to breathe through your nose. Your nasal cavity is a filter for dust and pollution, warms and moistens incoming air, and creates the resistance that fully expands your lungs. When your nose is blocked or congested, your breathing shifts to your mouth. Which feels like fatigue, mental fog, anxiety, and restlessness. Nasal congestion is super common, but not “normal.” Our modern air carries pollen, dust, smoke, and particulates that accumulate, even if you’re not sick and don’t smoke.

In yoga, a kriya is a repeatable physical action that produces a mechanical result. Brushing your teeth is a kriya. Nasal cleansing, “jala neti,” is another. Clear breath is the first kriya because breath is foundational. Breath is before posture, before meditation, before life. This is why yogis clean their nasal passage daily. But you don’t need to be a yogi to benefit from a neti pot.

How It Works

Fill your neti pot with warm water and a teaspoon of salt. Put the spout into one nostril, lift your arm, and tilt your head over your sink or shower. Saline water flows from your nostril, behind your nose and through your nasal cavity, then out the other nostril.

This flow removes all the mucus, dust, pollen, smoke and debris that collect in your nasal passage and in front of your brain. Adding salt is important because it reduces swelling along your nasal lining, which physically widens your airways so you automatically pull in more air each breath. The effect is immediate: your nasal passages feel open, breathing becomes easier and your head feels clearer.

Pro tip: do this in the shower.

Material matters. Plastic degrades in salt water and sheds particles over time. Which you don’t want near your blood-brain barrier. Ceramic and porcelain work, but are fragile and slippery when wet. Copper is durable, naturally antimicrobial, and has been used for water vessels for thousands of years.
