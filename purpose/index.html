I’ve been fighting for air since 1998. It started as asthma, then pneumonia, then a speech impediment. Breath was never abstract. It was my floor and my ceiling before I had the words for it or knew what it meant.

Over time, something subtly shifted. I learned to breathe into each sentence through high school speech & debate. I learned to breathe into composure through collegiate boxing. I learned to breathe into flow through jiu jitsu. I learned to breathe into stillness through slacklining. I learned to breathe into relaxation through yoga. Each chapter felt like another breath deeper.

Looking back, that progress never came from sheer willpower or any single technique. It came from paying attention. From learning that sometimes that only thing to do is to breathe deeper.

Somewhere along the way, I became aware of the gap between who I was on paper and who I was in my body. My first name fit the world I was operating in. My middle name, Mackley, surfaced when my breath was clear and actions aligned. It was my great-grandfather Sunny’s middle name as well. A light in our family.

You enter this world on a breath. You leave when breathing stops. Everything in between is shaped by how clearly and deeply you breathe. I backpacked across the U.S., through Europe, and into India, towards the root of yoga and meditation. In one of the original ashrams, yogis rinsed their nasal passages each morning at 4AM–before anything else. If you breathe clearly, then you live clearly.

Breath is not belief. Breath is potential. When breathing is shallow, life feels rushed and noisy. When breathing is full, thought and action align. You know this difference. You have a Mackley inside of you—the potential you breathe into being. MACKLEY exists for you to breathe deeper.
